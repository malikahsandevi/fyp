Consolidation occurs when alveolar air is replaced by fluid, pus, blood or cells. It appears as a relatively homogeneous increase in lung opacity that obscures underlying vessels, often with air bronchograms.

Common causes are pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, haemorrhage and aspiration. The pattern and distribution, together with clinical findings, help narrow the cause.
