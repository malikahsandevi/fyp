Pleural effusion is an abnormal collection of fluid in the pleural space. On an upright frontal radiograph the earliest sign is blunting of the costophrenic angle, and larger effusions produce a dense opacity with a concave upper border (meniscus sign) that obscures the hemidiaphragm.

On supine films, fluid layers posteriorly and may appear only as a generalised hazy increase in density of the hemithorax. Ultrasound or CT can confirm and characterise the fluid, and the cause (for example heart failure, infection or malignancy) requires clinical assessment.
