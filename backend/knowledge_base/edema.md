Pulmonary oedema is excess fluid in the lung. Radiographic signs of cardiogenic oedema include enlarged upper-lobe vessels (cephalisation), Kerley B lines, peribronchial cuffing, perihilar airspace opacities, and often pleural effusions and cardiomegaly.

Non-cardiogenic causes such as acute respiratory distress syndrome can look similar, so clinical context is needed to distinguish them.
