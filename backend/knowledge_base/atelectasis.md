Atelectasis is partial or complete collapse of lung tissue (a segment, lobe or whole lung) so that the affected area contains less air. On a chest radiograph it appears as increased opacity, often linear or wedge shaped, together with signs of volume loss such as displaced fissures, elevation of the hemidiaphragm, crowding of vessels or ribs, and shift of the mediastinum or hilum toward the affected side.

Common causes include airway obstruction (mucus plug, tumour, foreign body), compression from pleural fluid or air, and postoperative hypoventilation. Plate-like (subsegmental) atelectasis at the lung bases is common after surgery and is often mild.
